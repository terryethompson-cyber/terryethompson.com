2026 & 2027 Chevrolet Trax.
The cheapest new Chevy. Here's what it can't do.

The Trax is one of the least expensive new vehicles sold in America. Five trims, one engine, front-wheel drive only, on every single one. A refreshed 2027 is coming, but Chevrolet hasn't revealed it yet. Here's what's real, what's rumor, and who this car isn't for.

The Short Version

The Trax is Chevy's cheapest new vehicle, and one of the cheapest sold anywhere in the country. Five trims, one 137-horsepower engine, front-wheel drive only, no exceptions on any trim. A mid-cycle refresh is coming for 2027, new styling and a new dashboard, but Chevrolet hasn't revealed it, priced it, or confirmed when it lands. If you don't need all-wheel drive, this is hard to beat for the money. If you do, keep reading before you fall for the ACTIV trim's rugged looks.

What's Coming

The 2027 refresh isn't official yet.

Everything below comes from spy photos and industry reporting, not a Chevrolet press release. Treat the details as likely, not locked in.

  • New front and rear styling. Spy photos show an upright front end and a two-lamp headlight layout.
  • An updated dashboard. Reports point to a more open layout and a new 8-inch driver display.
  • The mechanical package carries over. Same 137-hp turbo three, same six-speed automatic, front-wheel drive only. No AWD expected.
  • No pricing, no order bank, no on-sale date. The 2026 is running an unusually long production cycle, which fits a later refresh, but Chevrolet hasn't said when.

The Lineup

Five trims. Two of them are the same price.

Every trim shares the same engine and the same front-wheel-drive-only drivetrain. The differences are all equipment and looks, and one of them is a trap.

Entry

LS

8-inch touchscreen, analog gauges. Usable, not stripped.

Skip it

1RS

Sportier looks, same screen and gauges as LS. Costs the same as LT, which is the better buy.

Best value

LT

11-inch screen, digital cluster, remote start, auto climate. Same price as 1RS. Point most people here.

Sport look

2RS

19-inch wheels, same 11-inch screen as LT. About $2,200 more than LT for wheels and badges.

Rugged look

ACTIV

Roof rails, 18-inch wheels, outdoorsy look. Still front-wheel drive only. Buy the look, not the capability.

Buffalo Reality Check

No all-wheel drive. Not on any trim, ever.

Every Trax, every trim, LS through ACTIV, is front-wheel drive only. No AWD option exists at any price. The ACTIV's roof rails and 18-inch wheels look the part, but it's the identical drivetrain as the base LS.

Good winter tires close most of the gap for cleared roads and an occasional bad commute. If you're regularly climbing an unplowed hill, or you've specifically been shopping for AWD, say so. I'd rather put you in a Trailblazer or Equinox than sell you a Trax you'll be unhappy with in February.

137 HP 1.2L Turbo Three 162 lb-ft of torque, six-speed automatic, front-wheel drive. The only engine offered, on every trim.
54.1 FT³ Max cargo volume Class-leading for a subcompact SUV. 25.6 cubic feet behind the rear seat, 54.1 with it folded.
38.7" Rear legroom Close to what you'd get in a midsize sedan. Genuinely roomy for the class and the price.

Chevy Safety Assist is standard on every trim: automatic emergency braking, forward collision alert, lane keep assist, and front pedestrian braking. NHTSA gives the current Trax 4 out of 5 stars overall. It is not an IIHS Top Safety Pick. Both are true, and neither is a reason to avoid it. J.D. Power's predicted reliability score is 84 out of 100, rated Great.

Pricing

2026 prices, since 2027 has none yet.

2026 MSRP, before the $1,795 destination charge: LS $21,700, 1RS and LT both $23,200, 2RS and ACTIV both $25,400. With destination, the LS starts at $23,495, making it one of the least expensive new vehicles sold in the United States.

Chevrolet has not published 2027 pricing. Use these 2026 numbers as your baseline, not a promise. I'll have real 2027 figures the day Chevrolet releases them, and I'd rather sell you an in-stock 2026 at a price we both know is real than take a deposit against a number nobody has published yet.

My Honest Take

Three situations, three different answers.

This is one of the easiest recommendations I make, once you're honest with yourself about the AWD question.

You want the cheapest way into a new Chevy

Buy the LT. It matches the 1RS on price and beats it on nearly everything that matters day to day: the bigger screen, the digital cluster, remote start, automatic climate. There's no reason to pick the 1RS over it.

You need all-wheel drive for winter

Don't buy a Trax, ACTIV included. Nothing about that trim changes the drivetrain. Let me show you a Trailblazer or an Equinox instead. Both offer real AWD, and I'd rather have that conversation now than after you're stuck on an icy hill in February.

You're thinking about waiting for the 2027

Know what you're actually waiting for. The mechanical package isn't expected to change, so you'd be waiting on new styling and a new dashboard, not more capability. Nobody, including me, can tell you exactly when it arrives or what it costs. If the current one fits what you need, I wouldn't wait on a car nobody has priced yet.
